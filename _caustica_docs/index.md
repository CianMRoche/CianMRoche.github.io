[Caustica](/assets/caustica/) is a tool for easily visualizing strong gravitational lensing, named after the term used by 17th century mathematicians for the curves onto which refracted light rays converge, which were capable of burning objects.

Caustica was built with heavy use of AI tools for code generation, and as a whole its calculations have not been peer-reviewed or independently validated. Where a component was cross-checked against an established reference it is noted in the relevant section (for example, the EPL lens was ported from and validated against [lenstronomy](https://github.com/lenstronomy/lenstronomy); see <a href="/caustica-documentation/lens-models/">§5</a>). ## Quick start

On a computer the window has three parts: the image with a strip of view controls above it, the **scene drawer** below the image, and the control panels on the right, chosen with a column of icons along the right edge. Phones use a single scrolling column instead, with a redshift timeline and a plane viewer for the scene; the phone way of doing each step is given after the computer one.

1. **Add a plane** with **+ Plane** in the drawer's header, which puts an empty plane behind the farthest one. The strip beside it is your line of sight: it runs from the observer (the circle at its left) out in redshift, ending at **z<sub>max</sub>**, which you set in the box at its right end (type a value, or drag sideways on the box to scrub; it never goes below the farthest plane, and placing a plane beyond it raises it), with a mark for each plane at its z (the selected plane's mark is ringed). Drag a mark to move its plane in z, or click it to select that plane; pointing at a mark lights up its card, and pointing at a card lights up its mark. Below the strip, each plane is a card, in order of redshift, and clicking a card selects its plane. The chevron at the right of the header (or the handle at the top of the drawer) tucks the drawer away to give the image more room. *On phones*, tap the redshift timeline under the image to add a plane at that redshift; plane markers sit on the axis (stacking upward only when they would overlap), drag one to move it and tap one to select it, and the **z<sub>max</sub>** field at the axis's right end sets its range.
2. **Pick a creation tool** from the thin column of buttons at the left of the window: each button is the shape of that object's marker: ▲ for a **Lens** (deflects light), ▼ for a **Source** (emits light), and ◆ for a **Hybrid** (both at once, shown as a purple diamond), or keys 1 / 2 / 3. An armed button lights in the colour of what it places. There is no separate select tool: click the highlighted letter again (or press **Esc**) to switch it off, and with no tool active a click only selects and drags, so you can move objects around without risking creating a new one. The trash button at the bottom of the column deletes the selected object. The tools and the ruler (<a href="/caustica-documentation/coordinates/"><a href="/caustica-documentation/coordinates/">§1</a></a>) take turns: arming one switches the other off.
3. **Click to place** an object of the chosen type: in a card's viewer, which places it on that plane (and selects the plane), or on the main image, which places it on the selected plane; a small label beside the pointer over the image names the object and the redshift it will land at. Both put it at the same sky position, since a viewer shows its plane's true (unlensed) positions over the same field as the image. Drag to fine-tune, either in a viewer or on the image. Only the selected card shows the plane's controls; the others show their viewer and redshift, and a press on one of their viewers selects that plane (placing there at once if a tool is active). Beside the selected viewer are the plane's **Redshift** box (type a value, or drag sideways on it to scrub; the cards and the strip re-sort as it changes), and **Clear** (key **O**) and **Delete** (key **X**). The **[** / **]** keys step the selection through the planes. While any tab shows the Delensed image view (<a href="/caustica-documentation/background-image/"><a href="/caustica-documentation/background-image/">§7</a></a>), the tool column also has a mask button (key **4**). *On phones* the **Scene** tab holds a plane viewer for the selected plane, the only place objects are created there: its **L / S / H** tools (plus **M** in the Delensed image view) and a trash button run down its left side, the **‹ ›** arrows (or a horizontal swipe not starting on an object) step through the planes, and a **Redshift** box sits at the top right, with **Clear** and **Delete** buttons under a "Plane:" heading at the bottom right.
   **Selecting several objects.** On a computer, **Cmd**-click (**Ctrl**-click on Windows and Linux) adds an object to the selection or takes it out, in the image or in a card's viewer, and dragging across empty space in the image draws a box that selects every object inside it (hold Cmd/Ctrl to add the box to the selection). Dragging any selected object then moves them all by the same offset, across planes too. Only moving is shared: the Object panel, Delete and every other edit act on the object clicked last, which the panel notes while several are selected. A click on one member without dragging keeps just that one, a click on empty space clears the selection, and **Esc** returns to a single object.
4. **Adjust parameters** in the **Object** panel (the triangle at the top of the right-hand icons; the **Object** tab on phones). For hybrid objects, separate collapsible sections appear for the lens and source halves, each with its own show/hide (eye) and delete buttons, so a single half can be toggled or removed while the other stays. The eye button excludes an object from the computation without deleting it. Copy the selected object with **Cmd/Ctrl+C** and paste a duplicate in the same place with **Cmd/Ctrl+V**. Scene edits (adding, moving, deleting, and adjusting objects and planes) can be reversed with the **undo/redo arrows** to the right of the title (or **Cmd/Ctrl+Z** and **Cmd/Ctrl+Shift+Z**); view settings such as field of view and colour mapping are not part of the undo history.
5. **The image panel** updates in real time. On a computer each view is a **tab** in the strip above the image. Click the showing tab to change its view from a menu, **+** adds another view as a tab, and the letter keys switch the current tab's view (**I** for the lensed image, see the shortcuts below). The menus group the views by the plane they show: the **image plane** (the sky as observed: the lensed image and the quantity maps) or the **source plane** (the Image number and Delensed image views). A source-plane view says so with a **source** tag on its tab. To see two views side by side, use the split button at the end of the strip, which opens an empty pane offering the views, or drag a tab onto the right half of the image; tabs can then be dragged between the two strips. Both panes react to the same edits (objects can be placed and dragged in either), and clicking one makes it the view that the panels on the right and the shortcuts act on. The showing tab and its image share one outline, like a folder tab; beside another view, the focused one is marked by a teal line along the top of its tab. The **link** button between the two views chooses what they share: zoom, the reference source redshift, the display toggles (legend, scale bar, object shapes, colorbar, stretch, time delays), the instrument, line art, and the background framing. All are shared at first; switching one off gives each view its own copy, starting from the current setting, so for example the two views can be zoomed separately or show two instruments side by side, and switching it back on gives both views the focused one's setting. Color maps are set per kind of view in any case. Exports (<a href="/caustica-documentation/recording/"><a href="/caustica-documentation/recording/">§10</a></a>) capture the left pane. Each view has its own critical-curve, caustic and marker toggles at the right end of its tab strip, so curves can sit on a quantity map while the lensed image beside it stays clean; **C** toggles the curves and caustics of the focused view (on phones the toggles are chips on the image, under the view menu that stands in for the tabs). **Zoom** with the mouse wheel or a two-finger pinch; the **scale bar** at the bottom right of the image tracks the angular scale as you zoom (1″, 2″, 5″, or 10″ depending on the field of view), and the **Field** section of the View panel accepts an exact FOV value. On touch screens, a **horizontal swipe** across empty image space cycles the displayed view (the lensed image and each quantity map, wrapping around); vertical swipes still scroll the page, and touching an object still drags it. The **Hide all overlays** switch in the View panel blanks the labelling annotations (position markers, legend, ruler, scale bar) and the on-canvas controls for a clean plot until switched back; critical curves and caustics stay, since they are lensing structure rather than labels (toggle those off separately if you want them gone). Use the **Export** tab to save a PNG, WebM, or GIF.
6. **Measure angular distances** with the ruler. The ruler is off by default: enable it with the **Ruler** toggle in the View panel, or just press **L** (which enables and arms it in one step). Its button sits at the bottom-left of the image panel; click it or press **L** to arm it, then click-and-drag on the image between two points: a line appears with its separation in arcseconds and position angle (e.g. `1.42″ · 34°`). Each new measurement becomes the selected item, and measurements are editable: click one to select it, drag its line to move it, or drag an endpoint to reshape it. Delete the selected measurement with its **trash** button or **Backspace**; clear all of them with the **×** button. Measurements persist when you toggle the ruler off. The measurement lines are captured in saved PNGs and recordings; the ruler buttons themselves are not.
7. **Save and load configurations** using the Save / Load buttons in the top bar, next to the preset dropdown (on phones they move into the top bar's **⋯** overflow menu, which also holds Docs, Tour, the shortcut reference, and the theme toggle, keeping undo / redo and presets on a single row). The file stores all planes, objects, and parameters along with the full view state, so a loaded config reproduces the same picture: field of view, active quantity, per-quantity color mapping, contour spacing, the critical-curve resolution, point-source grid density, and render scale, and the overlay and ruler toggles. Any setting absent from a file loads at its default value.

**Where the controls live.** On a computer the icons down the right edge pick what the panel beside them shows: **Object** (the selected object's parameters), **View** (the **Field** section with the field of view and the reference source redshift, Hide all overlays, the **Display** toggles (among them **Shapes**, which shows or hides, in that view, the dashed outlines of the objects whose own **Show shape** is on), the **Critical curves** display options, and the settings of the active view, such as Fermat contours, Image number spacing, or the Delensed Image pixel scale), **Colors** (the **Color map** of the active view, the **Field** color, the **Annotations** ink, and the **Line art** switch, which shows its options, palette and colors while it is on), **Background**, **Instrument**, **Export**, and, at the bottom, the gear for **Settings** (cosmology and quality). On a computer every section is shown open. On phones the tab bar has **Object**, **Scene**, **View**, **Export**, and a gear for Settings, and the View tab lists the View, Colors, Background and Instrument sections one after another, some of them collapsible. Where these docs say "the View tab", it means that list on a phone and the matching panel on a computer.
